Data  Analyst  -  Assignment  
Objective  
We  are  looking  for  an  analyst  who  can  do  more  than  write  SQL,  build  dashboards,  or  train  
models.
 
Your  job  is  to  determine  whether  a  business  claim  is  actually  true  when  the  underlying  data  
is
 
messy,
 
incomplete,
 
contradictory,
 
and
 
potentially
 
misleading.
 
You  will  be  given  approximately  12  months  of  collections  data  across  borrowers,  accounts,  
agents,
 
campaigns,
 
calls,
 
digital
 
interactions,
 
field
 
visits,
 
promises-to-pay,
 
and
 
payments.
 
The  business  currently  reports:  “Recovery  has  improved  by  11%  month-on-month.”  
The  leadership  team  is  not  convinced.  
Your  assignment  is  simple:  
Find  out  what  actually  happened.  
You  should  assume  that  some  of  the  data,  definitions,  attribution  logic,  and  historical  
reporting
 
may
 
be
 
wrong.
 
Business  Context  
The  platform  manages  collections  across:  
●  Human  calling  ●  Predictive/progressive  dialing  ●  IVR  ●  Agentic  voice  ●  WhatsApp  ●  SMS  ●  Field  visits  
Leadership  wants  to  understand  whether  operational  performance  is  genuinely  improving  
and
 
where
 
the
 
next
 
₹10
 
Cr
 
investment
 
should
 
be
 
deployed.
 
You  have  been  given  raw  data  from  multiple  systems.  
There  is  no  guaranteed  data  dictionary .  
There  is  no  single  source  of  truth .  
You  cannot  assume  that  the  existing  reporting  logic  is  correct.  
Your  Mission  
Answer  four  questions:  
1.  What  happened?  
Reconstruct  the  actual  business  performance  over  the  last  12  months.  
Determine:  
●  When  performance  changed  ●  What  changed  ●  Where  it  changed  ●  Which  metrics  are  genuinely  improving  ●  Which  metrics  are  misleading  
 
2.  Why  did  it  happen?  
Identify  the  major  drivers  of  the  change.  
Investigate  at  minimum:  
●  Portfolio  mix  ●  DPD  ●  Client  ●  Geography  ●  Language  ●  Agent  ●  Agent  tenure  ●  Campaign  ●  Channel  ●  Telephony  vendor  ●  Calling  time  ●  Attempt  frequency  ●  Borrower  segment  
Do  not  assume  that  correlation  means  causation.  
Clearly  classify  conclusions  as:  
Fact  /  Strong  Evidence  /  Correlation  /  Hypothesis  
   
3.  Is  the  reported  11%  improvement  real?  
Build  an  independent  definition  of  recovery  performance.  
You  should  challenge  the  existing  definitions  of:  
●  Contact  rate  ●  RPC  ●  PTP  rate  ●  PTP  kept  rate  ●  Recovery  rate  ●  Recovery  per  account  ●  Recovery  per  agent-hour  ●  Cost  per  ₹  recovered  ●  Channel  conversion  
Explain  why  your  definitions  are  appropriate.  
If  the  reported  11%  improvement  is  wrong,  quantify  the  actual  change.  
If  it  is  correct,  prove  it.  
4.  Where  should  we  invest  ₹10  Cr?  
Leadership  can  invest  the  ₹10  Cr  in  only  one  area:  
1.  Better  telephony  infrastructure  2.  More  collection  agents  3.  AI  voice  automation  4.  Better  borrower  targeting  5.  WhatsApp/digital  engagement  6.  Field  operations  
Recommend  one .  
Your  recommendation  must  include:  
●  Expected  incremental  recovery  ●  Estimated  cost  ●  Expected  ROI  ●  Break-even  point  ●  Key  assumptions  ●  Downside  scenario  ●  Confidence/range  around  your  estimate  
If  the  data  is  insufficient  to  make  a  reliable  recommendation,  say  so  and  explain  what  
additional
 
experiment/data
 
would
 
be
 
required.
 
Data  
You  will  receive  data  covering  approximately  12  months.  
Expected  datasets  include:  
●  borrowers  ●  accounts  ●  agents  ●  agent_sessions  ●  campaigns  ●  daily_targeting  ●  calls  ●  call_attempts  ●  call_dispositions  ●  whatsapp_events  ●  sms_events  ●  field_visits  ●  promises_to_pay  ●  payments  ●  vendor_telephony  ●  complaints  ●  account_status_history  
The  datasets  may  contain:  
●  Duplicate  records  ●  Missing  values  ●  Conflicting  timestamps  ●  Different  time  zones  ●  Inconsistent  IDs  ●  Late-arriving  events  ●  Changed  schemas  ●  Changed  disposition  codes  ●  Duplicate  payment  events  ●  Historical  records  being  overwritten  ●  Multiple  identifiers  for  the  same  agent  ●  Inconsistent  campaign  definitions  
Do  not  assume  the  cleanest-looking  table  is  the  most  reliable.  
  
Part  1  -  Build  the  Golden  Dataset  
Create  a  trustworthy  analytical  layer  from  the  raw  data.  
You  must  document:  
●  Source-of-truth  decisions  ●  Entity  resolution  ●  Deduplication  logic  ●  Missing-data  treatment  ●  Timestamp  treatment  ●  Payment  attribution  ●  Historical  changes  ●  Exclusion  rules  ●  Data-quality  issues  ●  Assumptions  
Show:  
Raw  Records  →  Rejected/Corrected  →  Golden  Dataset  
Quantify  the  impact  of  your  cleaning  decisions.  
 
Part  2  -  Data  Forensics  
You  should  actively  search  for  inconsistencies.  
At  minimum,  investigate  whether  the  data  contains:  
A.  Duplicate  payments  
Could  retries  or  ingestion  issues  be  inflating  recovery?  
B.  Attribution  errors  
Are  payments  being  incorrectly  attributed  to  the  latest  campaign  or  interaction?  
C.  Timezone  problems  
Are  calls  being  classified  into  the  wrong  hour/day?  
D.  Vendor  mapping  changes  
Did  telephony  response/disposition  codes  change  during  the  period?  
E.  Agent  identity  problems  
Does  the  same  agent  appear  under  multiple  identifiers?  
F.  Portfolio  mix  changes  
Did  the  business  acquire  a  fundamentally  different  portfolio?  
G.  Denominator  manipulation  
Are  unsuccessful  accounts  disappearing  from  the  population  used  to  calculate  conversion?  
You  are  not  being  told  which  of  these  problems  actually  exist .  
Find  them  if  they  exist.  
 
Part  3  -  Statistical  Investigation  
You  must  investigate  whether  observed  improvements  are  caused  by  operational  changes  or  
by
 
changes
 
in
 
the
 
underlying
 
population.
 
At  minimum,  investigate:  
●  Mix  effects  ●  Cohort  effects  ●  Selection  bias  ●  Survivorship  bias  ●  Simpson's  paradox  ●  Attribution-window  bias  ●  Time-series  effects  
You  do  not  need  to  use  sophisticated  ML.  
A  simple,  transparent  statistical  method  is  preferred  over  a  complex  model  that  cannot  be  
explained.
 
 
Part  4  -  Counterfactual  
Assume  that  the  business  changed  its  targeting  strategy  midway  through  the  year.  
Leadership  asks:  
“What  would  recovery  have  looked  like  if  we  had  not  changed  the  
targeting
 
strategy?”
 
Design  a  methodology  to  estimate  this.  
You  may  use  approaches  such  as:  
●  Matching  ●  Regression  ●  Difference-in-differences  ●  Propensity  scoring  ●  Uplift  modelling  ●  Controlled  experimentation  ●  Bayesian  approaches  
Clearly  state:  
●  Your  treatment  group  ●  Control  group  ●  Assumptions  ●  Confounding  factors  ●  Identification  strategy  ●  Limitations  
You  will  receive  more  credit  for  correct  reasoning  than  methodological  complexity .  
 
Part  5  -  Production  Analytics  Design  
Assume  your  analysis  will  become  a  production  system  used  by  the  leadership  team  every  
day.
 
Design:  
Raw  →  Staging  →  Clean  →  Golden  →  Feature  →  Metrics  →  Dashboard  
Explain:  
●  Data  contracts  ●  Primary  keys  ●  Metric  definitions  ●  Data  lineage  ●  Incremental  processing  ●  Late-arriving  data  ●  Backfills  ●  Data-quality  checks  ●  Monitoring  
●  Anomaly  detection  
The  design  should  be  practical  enough  for  an  engineering  team  to  implement.  
Deliverables  
1.  SQL  Repository  
Production-quality  SQL  containing:  
●  Data  cleaning  ●  Transformations  ●  Metric  calculations  ●  Analytical  queries  
Queries  should  be  reproducible.  
 
2.  Analysis  Notebook  
Use  Python,  R,  SQL,  or  a  combination.  
The  notebook  should  show  your  reasoning,  not  just  the  final  charts.  
 
3.  Golden  Dataset  
Provide  the  final  analytical  dataset  or  reproducible  pipeline  used  to  create  it.  
 
4.  Data  Quality  Report  
Show:  
●  Major  data  issues  ●  Detection  methodology  ●  Treatment  ●  Business  impact  
 
5.  Executive  Dashboard  
Build  one  screen .  
A  CEO  should  be  able  to  understand  the  situation  in  approximately  60  seconds .  
Do  not  optimize  for  visual  complexity.  
Optimize  for  decisions.  
 
6.  Executive  Memo  
Maximum  2  pages .  
Answer:  
What  happened?  
Why  did  it  happen?  
How  confident  are  we?  
What  should  we  do?

Show  how  your  analytical  system  would  work  in  production.
